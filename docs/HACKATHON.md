## About the challenge

PayPal already moves money for millions of people and businesses, every day, all over the world. This hackathon opens that platform up and asks what happens when you put AI behind the wheel: new agents, new ways to pay and get paid, tools nobody's built yet.

AI is changing how people build, buy, sell, work, connect and move money. *Build What's Next with PayPal and AI* is a global online hackathon for developers, designers, founders, students and builders. **There are no prescribed problem statements and no tracks.** Build an agent, an app, an automation, a new payment experience, a business tool, a social product, or something nobody has thought of yet.

**The one fundamental requirement: the project must meaningfully use both PayPal and AI.**

Why join:

- **Build something ambitious** — turn an idea into a working project using PayPal and AI.
- **Explore what's next** — experiment with new ways people, businesses and AI agents interact with money and payments.
- **Win part of $69,750** in total prizes — overall prizes and special awards across creativity, impact, execution and more.
- **Show what you can build** — put the project in front of PayPal judges, developers and the wider community.
- **Learn by building** — PayPal APIs, AI tools, agents, automations and new development workflows.
- **Build your way** — alone or as a team; start something new or meaningfully advance an existing project.

## Requirements

### What to build

Build or update an application that integrates the **PayPal developer platform (free sandbox environment)** with an AI tool, model or platform of your choice. Per the Official Rules this includes all PayPal services, such as Venmo, Braintree, Xoom, Honey, Zettle, Paidy, Hyperwallet, Simility and Swift Financial.

Optional sponsor tools: AG Grid, APIMatic, Astropods, Bryntum, Channel3, Elastic, KERNEL, Postman, Render, Zapier. PayPal provides access to select AI sponsor tools, but using them is not required. Any AI tool works, as long as **PayPal integration is central** to the project.

The project should:

- Meaningfully integrate at least one PayPal technology, API, SDK, product or developer capability.
- Meaningfully incorporate AI into the experience or functionality.
- Demonstrate a working prototype or proof of concept.
- Be documented well enough for judges to understand and evaluate it.
- Comply with the Official Rules.

Individual or team. New or existing projects are fine, as long as meaningful progress is made during the hackathon. An existing project must have been **significantly updated** after 1 Oct 2026, and the submission should explain how.

### What to submit

- [ ] **Project** built with the required developer tools and meeting the requirements above.
- [ ] **Text description** of the project's features and functionality.
- [ ] **Functional demo** judges can actually run or interact with, either:
  - clear, complete setup and run instructions in the repo, or
  - a hosted demo URL.
  - A design mockup, static prototype or non-functional demo does **not** qualify.
- [ ] **Tools**: name the models or AI services used and explain how each was used.
- [ ] **Public code repository** on GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket:
  - all source code, assets and instructions needed to run the project;
  - public and open source, with a **license file visible at the top of the repo** (About section).
- [ ] **Demo video**:
  - **under 3 minutes** (judges need not watch past that);
  - shows the project working on the device it was built for;
  - on **YouTube, public**, link on the submission form;
  - no third-party trademarks or copyrighted music/material without permission.

### How to stand out (organiser email, "two weeks to go")

- **Make PayPal and AI impossible to miss.** Name both in the description, the "Built With" section and the video, and show them working.
- **Give judges something to run:** a hosted demo URL or clear run instructions in the repo.
- **Hide API keys.** Use environment variables; never commit secrets.
- Submit by 5 Nov for the early eligibility check.
- Questions: the Devpost FAQs or the PayPal Discord.

## Judging criteria

Judging has two stages (Official Rules, section 6):

1. **Stage One — pass/fail.** Does the project reasonably fit the theme and reasonably apply the required PayPal APIs/SDKs?
2. **Stage Two — scored.** Five **equally weighted** criteria:

| Criterion | What judges ask |
|---|---|
| Technological Implementation | How thoroughly and skillfully does the project use the PayPal Developer Platform and AI tools? Is it a genuine, working, non-trivial implementation? |
| Design | Does it deliver a complete, coherent product experience, not just a technical proof of concept? |
| Potential Impact | Does it make a credible, specific case for solving a real problem for a real audience, and does what's demonstrated actually address it? |
| Innovation / Idea | How creative and novel is the concept, and how does it differ from existing ones? |
| Presentation | Does the video clearly show the project working end to end? Does the pitch say what problem is solved, who it's for and why it matters? Is it easy to follow? |

Ties are broken by comparing scores criterion by criterion in the order above (Technological Implementation first), then by a judges' vote. Judging may use expert panels, peer review, automated AI-driven analysis or a mix. Judges are not required to run the project and may judge on the description, images and video alone.

## Inspiration (from the organisers)

- **AI agents or automation.** Agents or workflows that use PayPal to act on a user's behalf — not just recommend, but actually transact. E.g. an agent that shops and checks out for you, a scheduling assistant that pays contractors automatically when a job is marked complete, or two agents negotiating and settling a payment between themselves (agent-to-agent commerce).
- **Money movement experiences.** A faster, simpler or more flexible way to move money. E.g. sending money with just a phone number, a dashboard of every payment a freelancer received across clients, instant payouts for gig workers when a task is approved, **splitting a group dinner bill automatically by scanning a receipt**, or a fundraising page that splits one donation across several causes.
- **Business solutions.** Tools for merchants, freelancers, creators, platforms or marketplaces. E.g. multi-currency invoicing for freelancers, tipping or memberships for creators, marketplace checkout that splits payment between platform and sellers, or a point-of-sale add-on for small merchants.
- **Consumer experiences.** Making it easier for everyday people to pay, get paid or track money. E.g. a budgeting app that categorises PayPal transactions automatically, a "request money" flow inside a chat app, or a simpler checkout for a niche audience (students, families, seniors).
- **Social impact.** Using PayPal to expand access or do good. E.g. an accessible, multi-language donation platform; microloans or fundraising for underbanked users; automated recurring payouts from nonprofits to field partners; or neighbours pooling money for a shared community or climate project.
